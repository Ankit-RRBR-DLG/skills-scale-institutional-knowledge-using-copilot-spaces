# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management documentation hub. This page is the central entry point to the processes, roles, and practices we use to plan and deliver cross-functional projects.

## Project Management Overview

OctoAcme uses a phase-gated project lifecycle that moves from initiation through planning, execution, release, and closure. Each project begins with a lightweight one-pager to validate the business need, define success metrics, and confirm stakeholder alignment before detailed planning. Teams then build a prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria, define the Definition of Done, and establish a release plan. During execution, work is delivered in small, shippable increments, supported by daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and demos at sprint or milestone boundaries.

Three primary roles provide clear ownership and accountability. Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and cross-team communication; Product Managers define product vision, prioritize the backlog around customer value, and own measurable outcomes; and Developers implement features, write tests, participate in design reviews, and identify technical risks. Together, these roles distinguish what to build, how to build it, and how to coordinate delivery while encouraging psychological safety and data-informed decisions.

Communication follows a structured cadence: daily standups focus on progress and blockers, Product and Project leads align weekly, delivery teams meet regularly (typically twice weekly), and stakeholders receive monthly updates. Teams maintain a Risk Register with each risk’s description, impact, likelihood, owner, and mitigation plan. Issues are addressed at the team level first, escalated to the Project Manager and Product Lead when needed, and taken to the Sponsor when business impact is critical. Standardized updates make progress, next steps, risks, and decisions visible.

Quality is built into delivery through unit and integration tests, CI security scanning, small pull requests (ideally no more than 400 lines) with at least one approval, and end-to-end smoke tests before production release. Releases include pre-release verification, staged deployment, post-deployment checks, and a documented rollback plan. At closure—and after significant milestones or incidents—teams hold structured, blameless retrospectives and prioritize two or three actionable improvements with owners and timelines.
